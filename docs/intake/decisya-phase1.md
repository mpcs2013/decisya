## Epics

### EP-01: Onboarding: first sign-in to the ledger

- Goal: A new person states the facts Decisya needs, accepts the terms, and reaches the ledger with an account ready for a first entry.
- Release: MVP, milestone 1 Ledger
- Planned: 2027-04-15 to 2027-05-01
- Actors: ACT-01, ACT-02, ACT-07
- Plan: free

Acceptance criteria (keyed by epic and position):

- EP-01#1: On first sign-in, after the enrolment of two authenticators \(EP-24 AC-24-3\), the person may state a coarse region of residence: Switzerland, EU, UK, other, or "prefer not to say". The question is optional and can be skipped. The region is never verified \(no address, no location or IP lookup\), never blocks sign-up and never changes what the product offers; its purposes are stated in the privacy notice \(EP-13 AC-13-1\). The person may also state a preferred language for future versions; English stays the only interface language. This is the only language question in onboarding: if the stated preferred language is English, German or French, the acceptance screen shows the terms and the privacy notice in it by default; otherwise, or if none is stated, in English \(AC-01-7 \(d\), EP-13 AC-13-3 \(d\)\). They accept the current terms and privacy notice on the acceptance screen of AC-01-7 \(EP-13\), and only then reach the ledger.
- EP-01#2: Before the first transaction, the owner chooses a main currency \(any ISO 4217 code\) and creates a first account \(EP-02\). Every step works by keyboard alone.
- EP-01#3: The transaction form says, next to the note field, not to type bank logins, card numbers or IBANs.
- EP-01#4: Once the household ADR allows invitations \(EP-10 AC-10-1, BL-F20\), a person who arrives through a household invite skips space set-up. They land in the inviter's space, with a plain statement of what a member can see and do \(EP-10\).
- EP-01#5: The stated region \(or that none was stated\), the preferred language and, for a sign-up with a beta code, the code's wave or channel label are kept in the person's profile, which closing the account deletes \(AC-12-5\). They are never written into the append-only audit and measurement records \(EP-14\), except as the closure event allows \(AC-14-4\). No user's postal address is asked for or stored, at sign-up or anywhere else in Phase 1.
- EP-01#6: \(BL-F27\) The terms and privacy-notice acceptance step of AC-01-1 and AC-01-7 is built, tested and closed with this epic on the marked placeholder texts of EP-13 \(AC-13-8\), with a marked placeholder in each of the three languages of AC-13-3, so the language choice of AC-01-7 \(d\) and the language stored with the acceptance are built and tested here. Bringing in the final texts after the narrow legal review, with their versions recorded \(AC-13-3\), and any rework of this step they need is EP-13's follow-up item, tracked on its own \(AC-13-8\), not a reopening of this epic. It is done before the first release to real users \(the invite-only beta\); no real user accepts a placeholder text.
- EP-01#7: \(Swiss UWG Art. 3\(1\)\(s\)\) The acceptance screen \(a\) states, before the person accepts, the steps that lead to the agreement: what they have entered, where to read the terms and the privacy notice \(linked, with their versions, in the language chosen under \(d\)\), and that accepting concludes the agreement; \(b\) shows what the person entered during onboarding \(the region and the preferred language for future versions of AC-01-1\) and lets them go back and correct it before accepting; \(c\) after acceptance, shows a confirmation in the application that names the accepted versions, the language in which they were accepted and the time, and the confirmation stays viewable in the account \(AC-13-3 \(e\)\); \(d\) offers the choice of the language of the terms and the privacy notice \(English, German or French, EP-13 AC-13-3 \(d\)\), set by default to the preferred language of AC-01-1 if it is one of the three, otherwise English; changing it changes only the language of these texts on the screen and behind its links, never the interface language \(NFR-08\); each language name in the choice declares its language \(AC-13-3 \(h\)\). EP-01 builds and closes the choice on the marked placeholders of AC-01-6 in all three languages, so EP-13's follow-up only replaces the texts. A confirmation by email is sent only if the legal review asks for one, and then as a notice of EP-27 with no link or code \(BL-C25\). The same screen serves the acceptance of a new version \(AC-13-3\). Every step works by keyboard and screen reader \(BL-C11\).

### EP-02: Accounts \(Tier 1: at most 2 accounts\)

- Goal: The owner keeps up to 2 accounts, each in its own currency, so every transaction has a home and a balance.
- Release: MVP, milestone 1 Ledger
- Planned: 2026-12-15 to 2027-01-16
- Actors: ACT-01, ACT-02
- Plan: free, gated

Acceptance criteria (keyed by epic and position):

- EP-02#1: The owner creates an account with a name, one ISO 4217 currency and an optional opening balance with its date. Amounts are kept in decimal minor units with the currency. No field asks for an IBAN or a card number. A member sees the accounts but does not create them.
- EP-02#2: Tier 1 \(Free plan\): a space holds at most 2 accounts. A third is refused with a message that names the limit, and the account list shows how much of the limit is used \(for example "2 of 2"\).
- EP-02#3: The owner can rename an account. Its currency cannot be changed once it holds a transaction.
- EP-02#4: Each account shows its balance in its own currency: the opening balance plus its transactions.
- EP-02#5: \(BL-F31; the business owner's decision on the limits\) The account limit of AC-02-2 is one fixed value in code, in the module that owns accounts; it does not come from configuration. It ships with this epic and holds without any lift: until a lift ships, it applies to every space, whatever its plan. The only thing that may ever lift it is one Pro feature key in the plan catalogue \(name per OQ-09; proposed \`ledger.accounts.unlimited\`\), which ships, with its check, in EP-03 \(AC-03-1\); no other key, plan or setting changes it. If EP-03 moves to the next phase, no key lifts the limit, and that needs no ADR, because no second mechanism is added \(BL-F31\). The server enforces it in the same transaction as the write that creates an account, so concurrent requests cannot pass it: a test sends parallel create requests to a space at its limit and checks that the space never ends with more accounts than the limit. The first issue that needs the limit designs it in its architecture note \(G2\). No second gating mechanism is added; if limits ever differ by plan \(a second paid plan\) or come from configuration, a Decisya ADR comes first \(BL-F31\).

### EP-03: Accounts beyond the Free limit \(Tier 2: Pro, the lowest paid plan\)

- Goal: A Pro space can keep as many accounts as it needs.
- Release: MVP, milestone 1 Ledger
- Planned: 2028-03-26 to 2028-04-19
- Actors: ACT-01, ACT-03
- Plan: pro, gated

Acceptance criteria (keyed by epic and position):

- EP-03#1: \(BL-F31\) Pro: one Pro feature key in the plan catalogue \(name per OQ-09; proposed \`ledger.accounts.unlimited\`\) lifts EP-02's account limit \(AC-02-5\), so a space on Pro has no account limit, and the account list shows none. The key and its check ship with this epic; EP-02's limit does not wait for them.
- EP-03#2: \(BL-F24, BL-F32, BL-C40\) In Phase 1, a space reaches Pro only through Decisya's built one-time trial \(BL-F5\) or an audited admin override \(BL-F5; BL-F24: "Until then the admin assigns plans"\), with no charge. No purchase path, price or payment step appears anywhere, so no Phase 1 epic charges anyone or touches a payment; whether a payment is a money-moving action under BL-C30 is settled by billing's own ADR \(OQ-25, OQ-25\(b\)\).
- EP-03#3: When Pro ends while a space holds more than 2 accounts, nothing is deleted or hidden, and all accounts stay usable. The owner cannot create another account until the space is back under the Tier 1 limit, and is told why.

### EP-04: Record transactions by hand \(add, edit, delete\)

- Goal: An owner or member records, corrects and removes transactions in a few steps, and each entry keeps who made it.
- Release: MVP, milestone 1 Ledger
- Planned: 2027-01-17 to 2027-02-19
- Actors: ACT-01, ACT-02
- Plan: free, gated

Acceptance criteria (keyed by epic and position):

- EP-04#1: An owner or member adds a transaction with account, date, amount \(income or spending\), payee, an optional category and an optional note. The currency is the account's currency, shown next to the amount \(the business owner's decision: one currency per account\).
- EP-04#2: The server checks every field against a schema: the amount in the currency's minor units, a calendar date, payee and note within set length limits. Payee and note are shown back exactly as typed and are never run as markup.
- EP-04#3: An edit changes the transaction and the account balance at once. A deletion asks for confirmation, and an undo is offered right after it; once the undo has passed, the transaction is gone.
- EP-04#4: A person can read or change only the transactions of their own space. A request for another space's transaction is refused.
- EP-04#5: Every transaction stores the person who created it and the person who changed it last, as the person's internal id, never a name or an email. EP-21 only displays this, and the per-person counts of EP-17 rest on the same internal id \(EP-14 AC-14-4\).

### EP-05: Payees \(create, rename, merge\)

- Goal: Payee names stay clean: one name per payee, easy to fix.
- Release: MVP, milestone 1 Ledger
- Planned: 2027-02-20 to 2027-03-08
- Actors: ACT-01, ACT-02
- Plan: free, gated

Acceptance criteria (keyed by epic and position):

- EP-05#1: While a transaction is entered, the payee field suggests the space's existing payees as the person types. Choosing one reuses it; a new name creates a payee.
- EP-05#2: Renaming a payee changes the name on all its transactions.
- EP-05#3: Merging payee A into payee B first shows how many transactions move and asks for confirmation. It then moves all of A's transactions to B and removes A, and writes an audit record that holds no payee names \(EP-14\).

### EP-06: Categories \(Tier 1: at most 20 custom categories\)

- Goal: People sort spending their own way with a simple label.
- Release: MVP, milestone 1 Ledger
- Planned: 2027-05-02 to 2027-05-18
- Actors: ACT-01, ACT-02
- Plan: free, gated

Acceptance criteria (keyed by epic and position):

- EP-06#1: The owner creates, renames and deletes custom categories; a member creates them. A transaction has one category or none.
- EP-06#2: \(the business owner's decision on the limits\) On every plan, a space holds at most 20 custom categories. The 21st is refused with a message that names the limit. The cap is a business rule of the module that owns categories; no feature key or plan lifts it. The server enforces it in the same transaction as the write that creates a category, so concurrent requests cannot pass it: a test sends parallel create requests to a space at its cap and checks that the space never ends with more than 20 custom categories. This is for consistency, like the account limit \(AC-02-5\), not plan gating.
- EP-06#3: Deleting a category asks for confirmation and states how many transactions use it. Those transactions become uncategorised; none is deleted.

### EP-07: List and filter own transactions

- Goal: At the end of the week, people see where their spending went.
- Release: MVP, milestone 1 Ledger
- Planned: 2027-05-19 to 2027-06-04
- Actors: ACT-01, ACT-02
- Plan: free, gated

Acceptance criteria (keyed by epic and position):

- EP-07#1: The list shows the space's transactions, newest first, page by page, with date, account, payee, category, amount and currency.
- EP-07#2: People filter by account, payee, category and date range, combine filters, and see the number of results.
- EP-07#3: Filter values never appear in the URL; they travel in the request body \(NFR-03\).
- EP-07#4: The table and the filters work by keyboard and with a screen reader, and every amount is read out with its currency \(NFR-07\).

### EP-08: Daily exchange rates \(system feed\)

- Goal: Every day, after the source publishes, Decisya has dated reference rates for the overview, and every stored rate belongs to one tenant.
- Release: MVP, milestone 1 Ledger
- Planned: 2027-12-17 to 2028-01-22
- Actors: ACT-08, ACT-09, ACT-11
- Plan: free

Acceptance criteria (keyed by epic and position):

- EP-08#1: Once a day, the feed gets the latest reference rates from the agreed source: the Currency Tracker or its replacement. Where it runs, its source and its terms are settled by the business owner before this epic starts. For each rate it stores the currency pair, the rate's own date and the source.
- EP-08#2: Until Decisya's architect answers OQ-06, the fetched rates are written straight into a copy per tenant. Decisya's database holds no rate table or staging copy outside a tenant \(BL-C1a\). If the Currency Tracker keeps its own store, that store is outside Decisya's database \(AC-08-1\).
- EP-08#3: The daily fetch runs after the source's daily publication time \(for the ECB, after about 16:00 CET\) and retries later the same day if it fails. If it still fails, the last rates are kept with their date. No tenant data is ever sent to the rate source.
- EP-08#4: Only the system writes rates; no user or admin screen can change one. Each fetch's success or failure and its date go to the built telemetry \(BL-B5\) as an operational log and metric, not to a database record.

### EP-09: Main-currency overview

- Goal: People see each account's balance and an indicative total in one main currency, with the rate date.
- Release: MVP, milestone 1 Ledger
- Planned: 2028-01-23 to 2028-02-25
- Actors: ACT-01, ACT-02
- Plan: free, gated

Acceptance criteria (keyed by epic and position):

- EP-09#1: The owner sets one main currency for the space \(any ISO 4217 code\) and can change it. Both members see the same overview \(the business owner's decision: one main currency per space\).
- EP-09#2: The overview lists each account's balance in its own currency. Where a rate exists, it also shows the value in the main currency and a total in the main currency. Every converted value is labelled "indicative" with the rate's date.
- EP-09#3: An account whose currency has no rate, or whose rate is stale under NFR-12, is shown in its own currency only and is left out of the total, and the overview says so. Days on which the source does not publish never make a rate stale. The overview never shows an undated total.
- EP-09#4: The overview shows balances only. It gives no advice and makes no recommendation.

### EP-10: Household \(Tier 1: up to 2 people\), conditional on a new Decisya ADR

- Goal: Two people keep one shared ledger, each with their own sign-in, and each knows what they may do, once Decisya's household ADR allows a tenant membership for another person.
- Release: MVP, milestone 1 Ledger
- Planned: 2028-01-04 to 2028-02-17
- Actors: ACT-01, ACT-02, ACT-07
- Plan: free, gated

Acceptance criteria (keyed by epic and position):

- EP-10#1: Nothing of this epic ships before Decisya's household ADR is accepted and carried in a superseding baseline \(BL-F20, BL-C33\). Until then no end-user account reaches another person's space, no invite can be created, and no product or help text claims a shared household \(AC-13-7 \(a\)\). The other stories of this epic are written at G1 from the ADR.
- EP-10#2: \(the business owner's decision on the limits\) On every plan, a space holds at most 2 people, the owner included. While a member is present or an invite is pending, a new invite is refused with a message that names the limit. The cap is a business rule of the module that owns memberships; no feature key or plan lifts it \(BL-F31's conditions apply only to a limit a Pro key lifts\). The server enforces it in the same transaction as each write that creates an invite or a membership, in whatever form the ADR gives the invite, so concurrent requests cannot pass it: a test sends parallel invite and join requests to a space at its cap and checks that the space never ends with more than 2 people, or with an invite pending while a member is present. This is for consistency, like the account limit \(AC-02-5\), not plan gating.
- EP-10#3: Each person in a space has their own Decisya account and signs in under EP-24, with their own two authenticators \(BL-C23, BL-C26\); no credential is created for, shared with or registered by another person. Granting tenant access follows BL-C30 as the ADR applies it \(BL-F21\), and is notified \(EP-27\).
- EP-10#4: The owner sees the same message and status code for every invitee and is never told that the person already has an account or another space \(6.3.8\); only the invitee learns of a conflict, after signing in. The invitee's sign-in or sign-up gives no sign of whether an account exists, as AC-24-6 sets out.
- EP-10#5: Each person can see a plain list of what their role can and cannot do, whatever the ADR sets. A member can leave at any time. When a person's membership ends, all of that person's sessions end \(7.4.2, EP-26 AC-26-4\), and the join and the leave are recorded as measurement events \(EP-14 AC-14-4\).

### EP-11: Basic CSV export

- Goal: Anyone in a space can take a full copy of their ledger, free, on every plan: in self-service after a device-bound step-up, or through the support procedure, with the path stated plainly before they start.
- Release: MVP, milestone 1 Ledger
- Planned: 2027-08-04 to 2027-09-05
- Actors: ACT-01, ACT-02, ACT-04, ACT-10
- Plan: free

Acceptance criteria (keyed by epic and position):

- EP-11#1: An owner or member downloads the space's transactions as a CSV file, either the whole ledger or the current filter. This works on every plan.
- EP-11#2: The file is UTF-8 with a header row. Each line holds the date \(ISO 8601\), account, payee, category, note, the amount as a decimal number and the ISO 4217 code. Text that starts with a formula character is escaped, so that spreadsheets do not run it.
- EP-11#3: The same download offers a second file with the accounts \(name, currency, opening balance and its date\) and the categories, so that the ledger can be rebuilt elsewhere.
- EP-11#4: The files hold only the requester's own space, and each export, in self-service or through support, writes an audit record without content \(EP-14\).
- EP-11#5: \(BL-C30, BL-F21\) Every CSV export, whole ledger or filtered, and the accounts and categories file of AC-11-3, is a sensitive operation. With a device-bound key, the person exports in self-service after the device-bound step-up of EP-26 AC-26-5; a further download needs no new step while that proof is at most 300 s old. Without one, the person asks for the export in the product after the passkey re-authentication of EP-25 AC-25-10, which opens a support case \(EP-25 AC-25-3\). When the case completes after its hold, the files are delivered inside the application, never by email: they wait in the requester's own space for the person who opened the case and no one else, who downloads them at a later sign-in after the same passkey re-authentication, during a download period Decisya sets and the notice states; then they are removed. The address on record \(BL-F21\) and the application get a completion notice with no link, no code and no ledger content \(EP-27 AC-27-4, AC-27-7\). No file is attached to an email and no download link is emailed, because BL-C25 allows email "only as a contact check inside the human support procedure and for notices" and AC-27-4 forbids ledger content in any email; whether BL-F21's "goes to the address on record" asks for delivery by email is OQ-23. Every export is notified \(EP-27\). The rule covers every export because any "near-whole" threshold could be dodged by several smaller exports. Both paths cost nothing and are on every plan.
- EP-11#6: \(positioning rule, AC-13-7 \(c\)\) Before the person starts, the export screen says plainly which path applies to their account: self-service after the device-bound step-up, or support with a hold of at least 7 days \(14 if the account ever held a device-bound key, read from EP-24 AC-24-10\), after which the files wait in the application and a notice goes to the address on record. It also says that a device-bound key can be added in self-service only at enrolment.

### EP-12: Data deletion: ledger account, whole space, closing one's Decisya account

- Goal: People can remove a ledger account, wipe the whole ledger or close their Decisya account, in self-service after a device-bound step-up or through the support procedure. Their ledger content and their profile leave the live system; what Decisya keeps after that, and why and for how long, including the grace period of a deletion through support, is listed in the privacy notice \(EP-13\).
- Release: MVP, milestone 1 Ledger
- Planned: 2027-09-06 to 2027-10-24
- Actors: ACT-01, ACT-02, ACT-04, ACT-07, ACT-10
- Plan: free

Acceptance criteria (keyed by epic and position):

- EP-12#1: \(OQ-26, strict default\) The owner deletes a ledger account \(EP-02\) after a confirmation that states how many transactions go with it. The account and its transactions are removed from the live database. Deleting the space's last ledger account, or an account whose deletion would leave the space with no transaction while it holds some, has the same irreversible result as a wipe and is treated exactly like the wipe of AC-12-2: the device-bound step-up, or a support case opened after the passkey re-authentication of EP-25 AC-25-10, restorable for its grace period \(BL-C37, EP-28 AC-28-7\). Any other ledger account deletion is a normal write: deleting a ledger account is not among BL-C30's six sensitive operations, and OQ-26 asks Decisya about both deletions together. Single transactions are deleted in EP-04, one at a time with confirmation and undo; Phase 1 has no bulk deletion of transactions, and any later one that would remove every transaction of a space is treated like the wipe.
- EP-12#2: \(OQ-26, strict default\) The owner wipes the whole space after a second confirmation that states what will go. A wipe is treated as a sensitive operation under BL-C30, on top of the fresh sign-in the business owner set for a wipe: with a device-bound key, the device-bound step-up of EP-26 AC-26-5, which also counts as the fresh sign-in; without one, the owner asks for the wipe after the passkey re-authentication of EP-25 AC-25-10 \(7.5.3\), which opens a support case \(EP-25 AC-25-3\) and runs when the case completes, restorable for its grace period \(BL-C37, EP-28 AC-28-7\). If a member is present \(EP-10, once its ADR allows it\), the member sees a notice and can export for 7 days before the wipe runs, and the owner can cancel during that time; the wipe runs when both the case and the member's period have ended. A self-service wipe of a space with no member runs at once. If Decisya answers OQ-26 that a wipe is not a sensitive operation, the fresh sign-in and the confirmation remain.
- EP-12#3: \(BL-C30, BL-C37\) Anyone can close their own Decisya account, which is BL-C30's "account deletion": with a device-bound key, after the device-bound step-up of EP-26 AC-26-5; without one, through a support case \(EP-25 AC-25-3\) opened after the passkey re-authentication of EP-25 AC-25-10, and then the closed account stays restorable for a grace period at least as long as the hold that applied. During the grace period the block is held in Decisya's own account state, and the person's identity stays enabled in the identity service, with its registered credentials: no sign-in to the closed account reaches its data or any action, except that Decisya's sign-in lets a passkey registered to the closed account reach the restore request page and nothing else \(EP-28 AC-28-10\); a completed restore lifts the block \(EP-28 AC-28-7\). A member's closing works like leaving \(EP-10\); an owner's closing runs the whole-space wipe above. When the closure takes effect, in self-service or when the support case completes, Decisya itself ends all of the person's existing sessions on every device at once on the server \(EP-26 AC-26-4, as EP-26 AC-26-6 ends sessions; ASVS 7.4.2\), and from that moment its own account-state block refuses every request from a session the account held before the closure. Decisya does not rely on the identity service's token refresh to end them: the identity stays enabled during the grace period and until its deletion is confirmed \(AC-12-5\), so a refresh would succeed. If a session or token of the identity service outlives that, how it ends is Decisya's design and its security review's call; no request reaches a page, data or action through it. The closure is notified \(EP-27\). An automated test closes two synthetic accounts, one in self-service with a device-bound key after the device-bound step-up and one through support, and checks for each: that every session the account held before the closure is refused at its next request after the closure, reaching no page, data or action; and that a sign-in started in the same browser after the closure that reuses a still-live session of the identity service without presenting a passkey reaches no page, data or action, the restore request page included \(EP-28 AC-28-10 opens that page only to a sign-in that presents a passkey registered to the closed account\). For the closure through support it also checks that, during the grace period, a sign-in with a passkey registered to it reaches the restore request page and no other page, data or action, and, after a completed restore, that the block is lifted.
- EP-12#4: After a deletion, an audit record states the kind of deletion, its path \(self-service or support case\), when, who \(the person's internal id, EP-14\) and how many records went, and a restore within the grace period writes its own record. It holds no payee, note, amount or account name.
- EP-12#5: \*\*Two moments of a closure.\*\* A closure is \*\*due\*\* once it can no longer be restored: at once in self-service, where it also takes effect at once \(the account is blocked and its sessions end, AC-12-3\); through support at the end of the grace period, or later if a restore request made inside it is still open, when that request is declined \(EP-28 AC-28-10\). It is \*\*completed\*\* once step \(2\) below is met. Where these criteria call a closure or a deletion "final", it means completed. From the moment it is due, the closure completes in this order, so that no identity is left that Decisya can no longer find. \(1\) Decisya deletes the person's identity in the identity service with its registered credentials. How Decisya reaches the identity service for this, through an administrative path of its own or one from the shared identity packages, is open \(OQ-12\). If the path Decisya uses cannot delete the identity, Decisya may instead disable the identity and remove its email, name and registered credentials, only if the narrow legal review confirms before the first real user that this is enough; the privacy notice then lists the disabled identity among what stays after closure \(EP-13 AC-13-5\). A manual operator step serves only as OQ-12 sets out. \(2\) The closure is completed only once the identity service has confirmed the deletion \(under the fallback, the disabling and the removal of the email, name and credentials\), or, where OQ-12 allows a manual operator step, once the support operator has recorded by an audited admin command \(EP-28 AC-28-2\) that the step is done. \(3\) Only then does Decisya delete the person's profile from the live database: stated region, preferred language, the beta code's wave or channel label \(AC-01-5\), terms acceptances, the account's "ever held a device-bound key" attribute \(EP-24 AC-24-10\), and any name or email Decisya holds; and it removes its own link to the identity last. Afterwards no record left in Decisya links the person's internal id to a sign-in identity or an email. \*\*From the moment the closure is due until it is completed, it is pending:\*\* if the deletion call fails, Decisya retries it; if it waits for a manual operator step, it stays open until the step is recorded; either way the support operator's queue shows the closure as pending, Decisya keeps its link to the identity, and the account stays blocked in Decisya's own account state as in AC-12-3, with no sign-in reaching any page, data or action and no restore possible any more \(a restore request gets the answer EP-28 AC-28-10 gives once a deletion is due\). The pending state is not a retention period and has no time limit of its own: it lasts only until the confirmation or the recorded step. Its bound, the closure's overall deadline, is AC-12-8. Each confirmation and each recorded manual step writes the deletion's audit record \(AC-12-4\). A closure stays in force after a restore from backup \(AC-18-6\). The test of the release candidate is AC-12-9.
- EP-12#6: The entries a closed member made stay in the household space and show "former member" as their creator \(EP-21\). The audit and measurement records of EP-14 stay, as listed in the privacy notice \(AC-13-5\).

### EP-24: Passkey sign-in, enrolment and sign-in details \(ADR-0025\)

- Actors: ACT-01, ACT-02, ACT-03, ACT-07, ACT-17

Acceptance criteria (keyed by epic and position):

- EP-24#1: \(BL-C22 to BL-C24, BL-C32\) Every end user \(role \`tenant-user\`\), owner or member, signs in with a passkey: a WebAuthn credential with user verification required, phishing-resistant and origin-bound, which a platform or password manager may sync through a cloud account. End users have no password. Sign-in depends on nothing beyond Decisya's own Keycloak.
- EP-24#2: \(BL-C25\) No SMS, voice, email-link, emailed-code, one-time-code \(TOTP\) or recovery-code factor exists anywhere, for any role, and no screen offers one. Email never signs anyone in and is never a factor \(6.3.6, 6.6.1\).
- EP-24#3: \(BL-C26, BL-F33, BL-F34\) At first sign-in, before onboarding \(EP-01\), the person enrols two authenticators, guided step by step in plain words. \*\*Enrolment is completed only when both are registered and each is verified as a separate working credential\*\*; the ledger opens only then, and the failed or unfinished case is AC-24-11. This outcome is Greenlight's interpretation of BL-F34 \("Each of the two authenticators must sign in on its own"\): BL-F34 states a property, and Greenlight takes it as a check per account at enrolment. How each is verified is Decisya's design \(OQ-38 \(d\)\): a sign-in by the person with each authenticator alone, or a system check without an extra step for the person if Decisya's review finds one that rules out a replaced credential on every provider. Greenlight's strict default until Decisya answers: a sign-in with each. That default adds up to two passkey ceremonies to every enrolment; AC-24-9 counts the step. Both may be synced passkeys \(BL-F33\). One synced passkey used on two devices is one authenticator \(BL-F33\), and a second passkey in the same keychain gives no second working key: it replaces the first or is refused \(BL-F34\). So the second authenticator comes from another passkey provider \(for example a password manager with its own account; on the same phone where the phone's system supports a second provider, AC-24-12\), another device whose keychain is not the first one's, or a security key, and the enrolment screen tells the person so before the second step \(BL-F34\), with the help of AC-24-12. The realm has "avoid same authenticator registration" on in the WebAuthn Passwordless policy, the policy under which end users register their passkeys \(Keycloak keeps the flag separately in its WebAuthn policy and its WebAuthn Passwordless policy\), so a second registration on the same authenticator is refused with a message that says what to use instead \(BL-F34; open until Decisya's realm issue confirms the setting and which policy end-user passkeys use on the pinned version, before the first real user, OQ-31\). An automated test registers two authenticators, then signs in with each alone, and fails unless both succeed; it also checks that a second registration on the same authenticator is refused. No screen says that a second provider protects against a stolen cloud account: each synced passkey depends on its own cloud account \(BL-F18; AC-13-7 \(b\)\).
- EP-24#4: \(BL-F21, BL-C28, BL-C39; positioning rule, AC-13-7 \(c\); OQ-28\) The enrolment offers, once, an optional device-bound key, as one of the two authenticators or a third. Before the person chooses, the screen says in plain words: with one, export, closing the account, a wipe, changing authenticators and changing the email or name can be done in self-service after a device-bound step-up; without one, each goes through support with a hold of at least 7 days; enrolment is the only self-service moment to add one, and adding one later is itself a support case; holding one makes every later support case of the account wait at least 14 days, and a recovery must re-enrol at the device-bound level or the account stays locked, for the account's whole life, also after the key is removed \(AC-24-10\). What counts as a device-bound key follows OQ-28 \(strict default: an attested device-bound authenticator\).
- EP-24#5: \(WCAG 2.2 SC 3.3.8\) Sign-in and enrolment rely on the device's own authenticator: no step asks the person to remember, transcribe or type a code or solve a puzzle. Every step works by keyboard alone and with a screen reader \(EP-20\).
- EP-24#6: \(6.3.8, 6.3.4\) Sign-in, sign-up \(with a beta code, EP-22; open sign-up; through an invite once EP-10's ADR allows it\) and their error messages give the same message, the same HTTP status code and no measurable difference in response time whether or not an account exists for the email typed. A sign-up with an address that already has an account creates no second account and changes nothing on the existing one, and that account's owner gets a notice without any link or code \(EP-27 AC-27-2\). An automated test compares the responses for known and unknown addresses, timing included, and fails on any difference. How the person at the screen is shown the same next step without a sign-up check link is Decisya's design \(OQ-27 \(b\), OQ-21 \(d\)\); this outcome binds either way. Every way in has the same strength and none is undocumented \(6.3.4\).
- EP-24#7: \(BL-C30, BL-C38, BL-C34\) A signed-in person changes authenticators \(adds, replaces or removes a passkey, or adds a device-bound key after enrolment\) and changes the registered email address or the registered name only with the device-bound step-up of EP-26 AC-26-5, or else through the support procedure \(EP-25 AC-25-3\), opened after the passkey re-authentication of EP-25 AC-25-10 \(7.5.1\), on every path: self-service, recovery, admin and API. The step-up never registers a credential, and no credential changes outside it or the support procedure: the account console's and application-initiated credential actions are disabled unless they pass through the step-up. This rule applies before and after enrolment is completed \(AC-24-3\); how it applies to a registration that fails its verification and to an unfinished enrolment is OQ-38, and until Decisya answers, AC-24-11's strict path applies. The only remaining authenticator cannot be removed. Each change is notified \(EP-27\), and the person is then offered to end all other sessions \(EP-26 AC-26-6\). A new email address is not checked by a link under OQ-27's strict default; it stays unverified until the first support case.
- EP-24#8: \(BL-C29, BL-C34\) Platform admins and the Keycloak master-realm operator account sign in only with device-bound, attested authenticators, two each, with no TOTP, before the first non-synthetic tenant, and \`/api/admin\` requires that level. If Decisya's realm issue finds that admin keys can only be enrolled under witness at the workstation, this reads "device-bound, enrolled under witness", as BL-C34 says. Where the admins register their two keys under Keycloak's WebAuthn policy rather than the passwordless one, "Avoid same authenticator registration" is on in that policy too \(the realm issue confirms, OQ-31\). How an admin recovers after losing both keys is OQ-21 \(e\).
- EP-24#9: \(counts\) The built telemetry \(BL-B5\) counts each week, as aggregate metrics without user id, email, IP address or any other identifier: sign-ups started; the first authenticator registered; the second authenticator registered; each authenticator verified as a separate working credential \(the check of AC-24-3\), which is \*\*enrolment completed\*\*; and enrolments completed with and without a device-bound key. Enrolment is completed only at the verified step, as in AC-24-3, so the drop between sign-ups started and enrolments completed includes everyone lost at the check, a person whose second registration replaced the first included. If the built telemetry cannot keep them that long \(OQ-08\), the admin copies the weekly counts out every week, before they expire. No database record is written for them. The account's durable device-bound state is AC-24-10, not these counts and not EP-14's events.
- EP-24#10: \(BL-C28, BL-C27, BL-C39\) Each account carries an attribute "ever held a device-bound key" \(yes or no\). It is set to yes in the same step that first registers a device-bound key to the account, at enrolment or when a support case adds one; it is never set back to no, removing the key does not clear it, and no user, admin, support or API command can change it. It is kept for the account's whole life, in the account's own tenant \(BL-C1a\) or with its identity, as Decisya decides \(OQ-24 \(b\)\), stays during a grace period, and is deleted only when a closure is final \(AC-12-5\). It is part of the backed-up account data, and its first setting is written to the identity change log, which is kept until the oldest backup it protects has aged out \(AC-18-6\), so a restore from any backup still held brings it back as yes. It is not an audit or measurement record: the retention of AC-14-5 does not apply to it, and EP-14's events may age out without changing it. The hold \(EP-28 AC-28-3\), the re-enrolment level \(EP-25 AC-25-4\) and the path statements \(AC-24-4, AC-11-6, AC-12-7\) read this attribute and nothing else, so BL-C28's 14 days and BL-C27's and BL-C39's original level hold for as long as the account exists.
- EP-24#11: \(BL-C26, BL-F34, BL-C34, BL-C30\) \*\*A failed or unfinished enrolment.\*\* Until enrolment is completed \(AC-24-3\), the account is not enrolled and the space does not open: no onboarding \(EP-01\), no ledger and no ledger data. A person who signs in to an account whose enrolment is not completed sees only where the enrolment stands and, in plain words, how to go on. - EP-24#12: \(BL-F34, BL-C26, BL-C11\) \*\*Enrolment help for the second authenticator.\*\* Before the second step of AC-24-3, the enrolment screen, and a help text it links to, say in plain words, with no brand promise: - on a recent phone system \(iOS 17 or later, Android 14 or later\), a second passkey provider can sit on the same phone: for example a password manager app that is free to use and stores passkeys. For a closed account, the person asks from the sign-in page \("restore my closed account"\) with a passkey still registered to the closed account: the identity stays enabled in the identity service during the grace period, and Decisya's sign-in, reading the block held in Decisya's own account state \(EP-12 AC-12-3\), lets that passkey reach only the restore request page, with no data and no other action \(tested as EP-12 AC-12-3 sets out\); with none left, through the recovery path of EP-25 AC-25-9. The page gives the same message, status code and timing whether or not a restorable account exists \(6.3.8\). The request is a support case of kind "restore" \(EP-25 AC-25-3\): a contact check through the registered email, which is still held during the grace period \(EP-12 AC-12-5\), and, under OQ-33's strict default, a hold as AC-28-3 sets out, announced by email and in the application \(for a closed account, on the restore request page to any of its registered passkeys\), where it can be stopped \(BL-C36\). A restore request received inside the grace period keeps the deletion restorable, and stops it from becoming due, until its case completes or is declined, so the grace period always covers the check and the hold; for a closed account, the completed case's restore lifts the block, and the account's registered credentials sign in as before; if the case is declined, the deletion becomes due at the later of the decision and the grace period's end, and a closure then completes as EP-12 AC-12-5 sets out. A restore is possible only until the deletion is due, not until a closure completes: a request made once a deletion is due, a closure that is still pending included, gets the same answer as a request about an address with no account. An email asking for a restore is answered with how to ask, and opens no case by itself. Each step is audited \(AC-28-2\) and notified \(EP-27\).

Session cookies, authorization headers, tokens, WebAuthn assertions and other credential material, and the content of support cases, never appear there either, checked by the same test \(BL-C8, Level 3\). Telemetry holding user ids, tenant ids or IP addresses has a retention period set by Decisya's architect. Admin output is counts and account facts only. No third-party trackers, and no cookies or device storage beyond what sign-in, the session, security and the person's own settings need, each on the factory's allowlist with its purpose and lifetime, and a test that fails on any key not on it; no analytics or advertising cookies; use is measured on the server only \(AC-13-4, AC-14-6\)

Terms and notice texts ship with the release; the sign-up gate's state and beta codes follow OQ-13; no cross-tenant total is stored. Cross-tenant reads happen only through audited admin commands \(BL-C1b\)
- NFR-05 (Auditability): Every action and measurement step listed in EP-14 writes an append-only record in the same transaction as the action \(BL-B1\). No audit or measurement record holds a payee, note, amount, account name, category name, email, person's name, beta code or code label, nor any session id, token or credential material, nor the text of a support request; the "who" is the internal person id. Every step of a support case is audited \(EP-28\). Records stay after a wipe or closure; measurement events are removed when the period of AC-14-5 ends \(the later of 39 months after the public launch and 12 months after the event; if sign-up has not opened within 39 months of the invite-only beta's opening, the beta's opening takes the public launch's place\), and audit records follow Decisya's own audit retention, both stated in the privacy notice \(AC-13-5\)
- NFR-06 (Availability and recovery): Availability of 99.5% a month, announced maintenance excluded, measured with the built telemetry \(BL-B5\). Recovery point at most 24 h and recovery time at most 24 h, proven by the restore drill on a fresh host before the first release to real users. The drill restores the identity service \(credential and session stores\) and the support case and hold records together with the ledger, and after a restore no earlier session stays valid \(AC-18-5\). Before sign-in reopens, every identity change and case step since the backup's time is re-applied from an identity change log kept outside the backup, so no removed credential, closed account or completed or stopped case comes back, and no hold is shortened \(AC-18-6, OQ-24\). Backups of the identity stores are protected as credentials. An acknowledged save is never lost silently: after a restore, every person is shown the time window that may have been lost \(EP-18, AC-18-4\)
- NFR-07 (Accessibility): WCAG 2.2 AA on every Phase 1 screen, checked automatically in the end-to-end suite and manually on the key journeys \(EP-20\), sign-up, the enrolment of two authenticators, sign-in, the device-bound step-up, the support path and recovery included. Authentication meets SC 3.3.8: passkeys, no code to remember or type \(EP-24\). The support procedure, its notices and the stop action work by keyboard and screen reader \(EP-25, EP-27\), and a session timeout is announced and can be extended \(SC 2.2.1, EP-26\)
- NFR-08 (Localisation): English is the only interface language in Phase 1. The privacy notice, the terms and the contact page are published in English, German and French for the beta, each language version covered by the narrow legal review; the person chooses the language of these texts, and that choice does not change the interface language \(AC-13-3, AC-13-14\). Each language version, and each language name in the choice, declares its language \(WCAG 2.2 SC 3.1.1, 3.1.2; AC-13-3 \(h\)\). All interface text lives in resource files, not in code, so another language can be added without code changes. Amounts are shown with their ISO 4217 code and the currency's minor units \(BL-C4\). Accounts accept any ISO 4217 currency. Booking dates are calendar dates without a time zone, and timestamps go through NodaTime \(BL-C5\). Dates are shown in an unambiguous form
- NFR-09 (Data rights): A deletion takes effect when it runs: at once in self-service \(after the waiting period of EP-12 where one applies\); through support when the case completes, after which it stays restorable for a grace period at least as long as the hold \(BL-C37\); a restore request made inside the grace period keeps it restorable until that request's case is decided \(AC-28-10\). When it takes effect, the ledger data it covers leaves the live database, and a closed account is blocked and all its sessions end \(EP-12 AC-12-3\). A deletion is due once it can no longer be restored: at once in self-service; through support at the end of the grace period, or when a restore request made inside it is declined, if that is later. A closure of a Decisya account then completes in the order of EP-12 AC-12-5: the identity is deleted first, and the profile and Decisya's link to the identity are deleted only once the identity service confirms that deletion or the support operator records an allowed manual step; until then the closure is pending. A closure's overall deadline is the response window the narrow legal review confirms for a deletion request, counted from the earliest day the person's request reached Decisya by any channel, as EP-12 AC-12-8 sets out \(where no earlier request exists, the day of the closure in self-service and the opening of the support case through support\); the hold, the grace period and a pending state all count against it, the pending state has no time limit of its own, and a closure still pending as that deadline nears is raised as overdue \(EP-12 AC-12-5, AC-12-8\). An export through support is ready in the application after the hold, for the person who asked, with a notice to the address on record \(AC-11-5; how this reads beside BL-F21 is OQ-23\). Backups age out within the retention period stated in the privacy notice. Export and deletion requests by email are answered within 30 days. Data-protection complaints to the controller are acknowledged within the period UK law requires and answered without undue delay \(AC-13-12\). Whether the hold \(7 or 14 days\), the grace period and a pending closure fit GDPR's one-month response window stays open until Decisya's recovery-runbook issue confirms it, before the first real user \(BL-F21\)
- NFR-10 (No advice, determinism): The ledger and the overview never depend on AI \(BL-C2a\); Phase 1 has no AI feature. No product text gives financial advice or recommends an action. Converted values are labelled indicative, with their rate date
- NFR-11 (Portability): Everything Phase 1 adds, the rate feed included, runs unchanged on Compose and K3s \(BL-C12\), uses only Postgres, Redis and S3-compatible storage \(BL-C13\), and fits one module per bounded context \(BL-C16\)
- NFR-12 (Rate data): Rates are fetched once a day, after the source's daily publication time, with a retry the same day. Each stored rate keeps its own date and source. Only the system writes rates. Where the source's calendar is not known, a rate older than 6 calendar days is stale. A stale rate is not used for a total \(EP-09\)

## Constraints

- Regulatory (European Accessibility Act, Constraint): WCAG 2.2 AA binds as a given limit of the baseline \(NFR-07\); whether the Act applies is not assessed here.
- Regulatory (EU AI Act, N/A): Phase 1 has no AI feature.
